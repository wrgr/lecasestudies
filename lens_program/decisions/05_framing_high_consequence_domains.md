# Framing Change: High-Consequence Domains, Representative Seven

*July 2026. A program-level framing change. Wider blast radius than anything else in this batch.*

---

## 1. The change

**From:** healthcare, defense, and education as co-equal domains.

**To:** LENS works in **high-consequence domains**, illustrated by a representative list of seven.

The category does the work. The list illustrates it and is explicitly not exhaustive.

**The representative seven**, following the structure the casebook's 205 cases actually took:

1. Healthcare and patient safety
2. Education, training, and the learning workforce
3. Aviation and aerospace
4. Defense and national security
5. Industry, energy, and enterprise systems
6. Disaster prevention and recovery
7. Algorithms, governance, and public systems

## 2. The criterion the category needs

A representative list invites the obvious question: *what makes a domain high-consequence?* Without an answer, seven is just a longer arbitrary list than three, and the first reviewer who asks gets silence.

**Proposed criterion.** A domain is high-consequence when the cost of a capability gap is paid by people who did not choose the risk, and the payment is not fully recoverable.

Read it across the seven: patients, students, passengers, warfighters and civilians, communities downwind, disaster victims, the governed. In every case the person who bears the failure is not the person who accepted the risk, and the harm does not undo.

Three things this criterion earns:

**It explains why education belongs next to aviation.** That is the argument LENS most needs and has never quite made. A student mis-served by a system they were assigned to did not choose the risk, and the years do not come back. Stated this way, the SOE audience is not being asked to accept that education is *like* the serious domains; education *is* one, on the same test.

**It draws the boundary honestly.** Retail training, sales enablement, and most corporate L&D fail the test: the cost lands on the organization that chose the risk, and it is recoverable. That is not a slight, it is a scope statement, and LENS is better for having one that does work.

**It is not about scale or drama.** "High-consequence" reads as *big* or *dangerous* unless the criterion says otherwise. It is about who pays and whether it undoes. That is why a two-person clinic qualifies and a large ed-tech rollout might not.

## 3. Why this is better than the triad

**Defense is de-pedestaled by arithmetic, not by framing.** The `lens_program/README.md` open items say: *"Defense is named as one domain among three (healthcare, defense, education), never the primary one."* That is a convention someone has to remember and enforce every time they write. Under the seven, defense is one of seven and some terms will not engage it at all — not because anyone sequenced it, but because the casebook is genuinely that broad. **The convention becomes a fact and stops needing enforcement.** For Bradshaw-era positioning this is a structurally better answer than a rule about word order.

**It matches where the cases are.** 205 cases fell into seven parts. The triad was a claim; the seven is a finding. LENS spent a casebook discovering the actual shape of the space and then described itself with a shape it had before.

**It widens recruiting without diluting.** Aviation, energy, emergency management, and public-systems professionals currently read the triad and conclude LENS is not for them. All four have the operator-system coupling problem in its sharpest form.

**"Representative" removes a defense obligation.** With three named as the domains, every omission is a question. With seven named as representative, the criterion answers the question and the list does not have to.

## 4. The tension this creates, and it is real

**De-pedestaling defense also de-pedestals education**, and LENS lives in a School of Education. Under the triad, education was one of three. Under the seven it is one of seven. A dean asking what makes this an education program gets a weaker answer from arithmetic than from the triad.

**The resolution, which should be explicit rather than implicit.** Education is not one domain among seven in LENS. It is both a domain *and* the disciplinary home. Every student is in an M.Ed.; the learning sciences are the foundation the whole concentration is built on; the field LENS extends is learning engineering. The seven are where capability work happens. Education is where LENS stands while doing it.

That is a stronger claim than "education is one of three," and it survives the arithmetic. But it has to be written down, because "education is one of seven" is what a reader will otherwise take away.

**Recommendation:** the framing paragraph names the category, gives the criterion, lists the seven, and then says in one sentence that education is both a domain on the list and the discipline LENS works from. One sentence, and it should appear in doc 2 §1, in dean-facing material, and in the recruitment email.

## 5. One caveat about the seven's provenance

The seven are the casebook's organizing parts. That is a strength — the list is empirically where 205 cases landed rather than an a priori taxonomy — and it is worth knowing that the program's framing now inherits an editorial decision made for a book. If the casebook reorganizes, the framing moves with it or the two drift apart.

Two options: treat the seven as the casebook's list and cite it as such (honest, and explains the provenance), or lift the seven into doc 2 as the program's own and let the casebook follow. The first is cheaper and more accurate. The second is more stable. Recommend the first, with the criterion as the thing that is actually load-bearing, since the criterion survives a casebook reorganization and the list does not.

### Recruitment email

Three instances, and they are the highest-visibility copy LENS has:

- *"Across healthcare, defense, and education, the question is the same..."*
- *"...in healthcare, defense, and education alike."*
- Verify note: *"Per program convention, defense is named as one domain among three, never the primary one."*

The first two get the category and a shorter illustrative cut — a recruitment email should not list seven. Something like *"Across healthcare, aviation, defense, energy, and public systems, the question is the same."* Five reads as a category; seven reads as an inventory. The verify note's convention is retired: it existed to police a ratio that the new framing produces on its own.

**The LEN 6 cost is worth stating plainly.** LEN 1 Week 13 rotates for the price of scheduling a practitioner. LEN 6 rotates for the price of a problem brief and an evidence packet per domain. Seven domains is seven packets, built once and reused, but it is real work and it is not this term's work. A defensible interim: LEN 6 keeps three fixed cycles for the founding cohort and the framing says the three are *drawn from* the seven rather than *being* the domains. That is honest and it does not block the launch.

## 7. Sequence

This is the largest-radius change in the batch and the cheapest to get wrong by half-applying. Doc 2 §1 is the anchor: everything else is a pointer to that paragraph. Write it first, get the criterion right, then let the other edits inherit it.

It rides the same doc 2 → v2.4 revision and the same program-owner round-trip as everything else. It is not a separate conversation, but it is the part of that conversation that needs the most air: the other edits are corrections, and this one is a position.
