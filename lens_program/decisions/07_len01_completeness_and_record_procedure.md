## 2.1 Failure methods, not the taxonomy — resolved

**Decision (July 2026): Module 5 teaches failure analysis as a set of established methods, not as a single scheme.** Students select a method, justify it against the case, and are assessed on the justification as much as the coding. The casebook's six-mode code is read as one instance, not as the standard.

This closes a dependency and improves the course in the same move.

**What it closes.** The taxonomy needed writing out before three graded memos and a 30% final could be scored, and it was the highest-priority gap in the course. It no longer gates anything. The six modes still work as a casebook index; they just are not load-bearing for assessment.

**What it improves, and this is the bigger half.** Week 11's Readings were "selected post-mortems; failure-code taxonomy" and its Additional Reading was Blake-Plock et al. (2023) at the GIFT Users Symposium — a narrow venue, and a paper *about* GIFT rather than an analysis of its adoption failure. It was one of the six weeks flagged as below the bar. Week 11 now reads:

- **Readings:** Reason (2000), *BMJ*; Hollnagel (2014), *Safety-I and Safety-II*, selections; selected post-mortems.
- **Additional Reading:** Leveson (2004), *A new accident model for engineering safer systems*, *Safety Science*.

All three are heavily cited peer-reviewed work, and Leveson (2004) is the systems-theoretic argument that component-failure models miss sociotechnical accidents. That is Week 11's thesis, sourced. **Six below-bar weeks became four.** Reason and Hollnagel join the Required Bibliography.

**One thing it costs, worth naming.** A bespoke taxonomy applied uniformly produces comparable coding across a cohort; method-of-choice does not. If cross-case comparison matters for the attainment profile, the memos need a common reporting frame even where the method varies. The rubric can carry that; the syllabus does not need to.

**It also previews LEN 3 rather than duplicating it.** LEN 3 Week 8 is the safety-science week (STAMP, STPA, Safety-II, HRO, Leveson's book). LEN 1 Week 11 now introduces the same intellectual move at paper grain and hands it forward. That is the sequence working as designed.

## 2.2 Week 13: engagements, not briefings — resolved

**Decision (July 2026): Week 13 is three asynchronous practitioner engagements with three of the seven core domains, rotating.** The seven, from the casebook's structure:

1. Healthcare and patient safety
2. Education, training, and the learning workforce
3. Aviation and aerospace
4. Defense and national security
5. Industry, energy, and enterprise systems
6. Disaster prevention and recovery
7. Algorithms, governance, and public systems

The three rotate by term and by the domain composition of the enrolled cohort.

**Three things this fixes at once.**

*Production becomes recruiting.* The heaviest item in the course was producing three video panels. It is now scheduling three practitioners. Async, repeatable, and the cost falls each term rather than being paid once at high quality and then aging.

*The co-equal-domains claim gets stronger, not weaker.* Week 13 previously named healthcare, defense, and education — the program triad, with defense one of three. Against seven domains, defense is one of seven, and any given term may not engage it at all. For the Bradshaw-era positioning this is a better structure than the triad: it de-pedestals defense by arithmetic rather than by framing, and it does so because the casebook is genuinely that broad, not because anyone sequenced it that way.

*The cohort drives it.* LENS treats students' prior domain expertise as an asset (doc 2 §1). Selecting engagement domains against the cohort's composition operationalizes that in the one week where it can be operationalized.

**What has to happen.** A practitioner roster across the seven domains, an engagement format that works asynchronously (recorded Q&A against student-submitted questions is the low-cost version), and a rotation rule so the selection is principled rather than opportunistic. Week 13's case selections follow the domains engaged, so the case slots for that week are chosen per term, not once.

**Resolved at program level.** The triad is retired. LENS works in high-consequence domains, with the seven as a representative list and a criterion that defines the category. See `LENS_Framing_High_Consequence_Domains.md`. LEN 1's Course Notes carry it already.

## 3. One thing worth reconsidering before the notes get written

**The flywheel's sourcing.** Week 9 teaches the evidence-capability-performance flywheel from a course note plus Wagner (2024), which is a practitioner blog series. Its Additional Reading is Van Campenhout et al. (2023), which is peer-reviewed but is about a progress panel, not about the flywheel. The flywheel is a load-bearing LENS concept and it is now the least-sourced week in the course.

Two honest options: write the course note as an argument that makes its own case and says plainly that the field literature has not tested it, or find the evidence. Week 12 has the same shape — the governance-objection diagnostic has no paper that analyzes what the week teaches. Noted in `LEN01_CLO_MLO_Record.md` §4: that is a research agenda, not a syllabus problem. But the course notes are where it becomes visible, so decide the posture before writing them rather than after.

Week 11 had this problem an hour ago and no longer does, which is the argument for looking hard at the other two.

# Part II — Applying the changes to the record

## 4. Yes: the repo is the record, and everything flows from it

`github.com/wrgr/lens-concentration/lens_program/` is the source of record for syllabi, capstone rubrics, casebook anchors, and recruiting. Nothing downstream should be edited to match a decision until the record carries it, or the next session inherits the same drift this thread just spent its time undoing.

The dependency runs one way:

```
lens_program/*.md          ← the record. Edit here first.
  ├── lens_program/*.docx      regenerated from the .md
  ├── casebook lens-anchor     already on v2.1
  ├── the ten syllabi          LEN 1 done; LEN 2–10 pending
  ├── capstone rubrics
  ├── published casebook PDF   still on v1 names (README flags it)
  └── capabilitymatters.org
```

## 5. The procedure the repo already sets

Docs 1 and 2 carry their own conventions. Follow them rather than inventing a process:

1. **Edit the `.md`, never the `.docx`.** The `.docx` is regenerated. The README says so.
2. **Bump the version header.** `<!-- LENS-VERSION: X | STATUS: CURRENT | ADOPTED: YYYY-MM | SUPERSEDES: ... -->`
3. **Add a Change Log entry** at the bottom of the doc. Docs 1 and 2 both keep one; prior prose lives in git history, and there is no `legacy/` folder by design.
4. **Regenerate the `.docx`.**
5. **Program-owner round-trip.** Diamond. Already open on v2.1/v2.2/v2.3 — these edits should join it rather than opening a second one.
6. **Then** propagate downstream.

## 8. What not to do

**Do not find-and-replace the tags.** v1 3.4 goes to **4.5**, not 4.4, because v2.1 inserted "judgment under inadequate evidence" at 4.4. Nine instances across LEN 4, 6, 9, and 10 silently land on a different competency if the domain digit is simply bumped. The per-location tables in the renumber document exist for this reason.

**Do not edit the syllabi before the record.** LEN 1 is a deliberate exception and it says so in its own footnote: it carries PLO 4.2 with a dagger recording that doc 2 has not caught up. That is a visible divergence with a stated status, which is different from silent drift.

**Do not open a second round-trip.** The v2.1/v2.2/v2.3 review is already outstanding with the program owner. Adding v2.2 of doc 1 and v2.4 of doc 2 to it costs one conversation. Sending them separately costs three.
